How Callback Coach works

Callback Coach is a rehearsal room for tech job interviews. You practice answering out loud against a clock, compare your answer with a strong sample, rate yourself honestly, and watch your scores climb from one session to the next. A few focused sessions a week will make the real call feel familiar.

A good 20-minute session

You don't have to use every tab every time. This routine covers the basics.

1

Warm up · 3 min

Answer "Tell me about yourself" in Question cards → Opening. It's the first question in almost every interview.

2

Rehearse · 10 min

Do 4–5 cards in the category that matches your next interview. Behavioral cards are the most common in hiring-manager rounds.

3

Check the basics · 4 min

Take a Quick quiz, or try one Code warm-up if you're applying for engineering roles.

4

Save your session · 1 min

Open Progress and click End & save session. Next time you'll see how each department moved.

Practice

Question cards

  • Pick a category with the chips at the top. The number shows how many cards you've nailed
  • Press Start 90s and answer out loud. Jot key points in the box if it helps
  • For behavioral cards, tick off Situation, Task, Action and Result as you cover them
  • Press Show sample answer, compare, then rate yourself

Cards you rate "Practice more" come back sooner. The colored squares under the card show your ratings for the deck.

Practice

Quick quiz

Ten random multiple-choice questions on terms recruiters and online tests check: APIs, HTTP status codes, SQL, Git and AI. Each answer shows a short explanation, so a miss still teaches you something. Your best score is kept.

Practice

Code warm-ups

Short JavaScript and SQL problems like the ones on HackerRank tests. Start the timer, write your solution in the box while talking through it, and use Show hint or Show solution if you're stuck. Press Mark solved when you're done. Your code and time are saved to your session.

Simulate

Mock pre-screen

A seven-question recruiter call for the role you choose, in the order a real call usually follows. The 90-second clock starts on its own for each question. Answer out loud, peek at the sample if you need to, then rate yourself to save the answer and move on. Recording yourself on your phone helps a lot.

Sessions and scores

A session is one sitting of practice. Everything you rate, finish or solve is added to the current session until you end it. Give it a name if it's prep for a specific interview.

Interview questions score 100 for Nailed it, 60 for Getting there and 20 for Practice more. Each department uses your latest rating per question.

Quick quiz scores the percent you got right, averaged across the quizzes in the session.

Code warm-ups score 100 if you solved it alone, 70 if you used the hint and 40 if you viewed the solution.

Arrows in the growth grid compare each score with the last session that practiced that department. ▲ means you improved.

Rate honestly. The scores only help if they're real. A "Practice more" today makes the jump to "Nailed it" next week easy to see.

XP, levels and streaks

You earn XP for every rep: 10 / 5 / 2 for a rated answer, 5 per correct quiz answer, 15 per solved problem and 25 for finishing a mock screen. XP moves you up through six levels:

Extra → Understudy (60) → Ensemble (180) → Featured (360) → Lead (600) → Headliner (900)

Your day streak counts the days in a row you've practiced. Short daily practice beats one long cram.

Common questions

Do I have to type my answers? No. Saying them out loud is the real practice. Notes are optional, but they let you compare your wording between sessions.

How do I make the answers mine? Log in and open My profile. Your details fill into every answer, and you can save your own version of any answer from the question card.

I ran over 90 seconds. The clock keeps counting in red so you can see by how much. Aim to finish under the limit.

Can I redo a question in the same session? Yes. Your latest rating for that question is the one that counts toward the score.

When should I end a session? When you stop for the day, or before a session aimed at a specific interview.

Can I use it on my phone? Yes. Open the site in your phone's browser and use "Add to Home Screen" to get an app icon that opens full screen.

I forgot my password. Click Log in, enter your email and choose Forgot password? to get a reset link.

I clicked the wrong button. Look for Undo in the message at the bottom of the screen. It appears for a few seconds after you rate an answer, end or delete a session, delete or save an answer, or clear your profile.

Can I fix a quiz answer? Yes. Choose Change my answer before moving on, or Start over. At the end you can Retake these questions, review them, or choose Don't count this quiz.

Can I delete a session? Yes. Open it under Progress → Saved sessions and click Delete this session.

How tech hiring actually works

Most tech roles move through the same five or six stages. Each one checks something different, so prepare for the stage you're in. The practice tabs map to these stages.

1

Application & resume screen

Checks: keywords and a clear story. Many companies use applicant tracking systems that scan for words from the job post.

Prep: mirror the post's language on your resume, put your best project () near the top with a link, and keep LinkedIn and GitHub current. A referral from a real person beats a cold application by a wide margin, so ask classmates, alumni and former coworkers.

2

Recruiter pre-screen · 20–30 min, phone or video

Checks: communication, basic fit, motivation, salary range, timeline, work authorization and location.

Prep: a 60-second "tell me about yourself," a researched "why this company," your salary range, and your start date. Practice with the Mock pre-screen tab.

3

Hiring manager interview · 30–60 min

Checks: whether you can do this specific job and work well with this team. Mostly behavioral and situational questions.

Prep: five or six STAR stories that cover customers, conflict, failure, leadership, learning fast and teaching. Practice in Question cards → Behavioral.

4

Technical or skills assessment

Checks: the hands-on skills in the post. This can be a timed online test (HackerRank, CodeSignal), a take-home project, a live exercise, a mock customer call, a product demo or a training sample.

Prep: for engineering, timed easy problems three times a week (Code warm-ups). For customer success, solutions or enablement roles, practice explaining a technical concept to a non-technical person and running a 10-minute demo of your own project.

5

Final round or panel · 2–4 hours

Checks: consistency across several interviewers, plus "would I want to work with this person every day?"

Prep: different stories for different interviewers, since they compare notes. Bring two or three questions for each person. Eat beforehand and keep water nearby.

6

Offer & negotiation

Checks: nothing. You've already won the job, and now it's a conversation.

Prep: it's normal and expected to ask for time and to negotiate politely. See the negotiation notes below.

Before every interview

  • Read the job post twice and pick the 3 skills they care about most
  • Study the company site, recent news and their product. Try the product if you can
  • Look up your interviewer on LinkedIn
  • Match one of your stories to each of those 3 skills
  • Write down 3 questions to ask them
  • Find the pay range (NYC posts must list one) and set your number
  • Video calls: test camera, mic and lighting. Plain background, laptop at eye level
  • Keep your resume and a few notes open, but don't read from them

During the interview

  • Answer in 1–2 minutes, then stop. Offer more: "Happy to go deeper on that"
  • Say "I" for what you did and "we" for the team result
  • Use real numbers where you can: team size, years, customers served, results
  • It's fine to pause: "Good question, let me think for a second"
  • If you don't know a technical answer, say so and explain how you'd find out
  • Never criticize a former employer or teammate
  • Close by repeating why you're excited about this specific role

Common mistakes that cost offers

Rambling. Long answers lose the listener. Use STAR and time yourself.

Generic "why us." If the answer fits any company, it fits none. Name something specific.

Underselling a career change. Hospitality, teaching and performing are real experience in client management, training and composure under pressure. Translate them; don't apologize for them.

No questions at the end. It can read as low interest. Always have three ready.

Bluffing technical answers. Interviewers can tell. "I haven't used that yet, but here's how I'd learn it" scores better.

No follow-up. Send a thank-you within 24 hours. Few candidates do, so it stands out.

Subject: Thank you — [Role] interview

Our conversation made me even more excited about the role. My experience in  and building  lines up well with [a need they described], and I'd love to help with that.

Please let me know if there's anything else I can share. I look forward to hearing about next steps.

Negotiating an offer

  • Thank them and say you're excited, then ask for a few days to review it
  • Ask for the full offer in writing: base pay, bonus, equity, benefits, PTO and start date
  • Compare with the posted range and market data before responding
  • Ask once, politely, with a reason: "Based on the range and my experience in X, would you consider [number]?"
  • If base pay is fixed, ask about a sign-on bonus, start date, learning budget or title
  • Get any final agreement in writing before you resign or accept

Handling the career-change question

Expect some version of "You're new to tech, so why should we hire you?" Answer it confidently in three beats:

  1. Own it: "I'm newer to tech, but not new to work."
  2. Translate it: "In I [trained staff / managed a team / handled hundreds of customers]. That's client management and enablement."
  3. Prove it: "And I've shipped real work, like ."
